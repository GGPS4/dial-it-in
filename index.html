      Why Spark 1 presets go wrong

      Most presets online are made on headphones, on a Spark 2, or by copying real-amp settings. The Spark 1 is a different instrument from any of those. These are the rules every recall on this site follows.

      It's two 4-inch speakers, not a guitar cab

      A 12" guitar speaker rolls off hard above 5 kHz. The Spark's full-range drivers don't, so treble settings copied from a real amp come out harsh. Treble on these recalls rarely goes past 6.

      The gain knobs run hot

      Spark models break up earlier than the amps they're based on. "Back in Black" is Gain 4.5, not 8. Most fizzy presets get fixed just by halving the gain.

      Don't scoop the mids

      Small speakers already sound boxy when the mids are pulled out. The metal "smile" setting turns to fizz and mush on a 2x4. Mid stays at 5 or above unless the record really was scooped, like Dimebag's.

      The top-panel knobs aren't motorized

      When you load a preset, the physical knobs don't move. Touch one and its value jumps to wherever the knob is pointing. Set the amp's knobs to match the panel at the top of this page, or leave them alone.

      Your pickups change everything

      A preset made on EMGs sounds weak on a Strat, and one made on a Strat sounds like mud on a Les Paul. Every recall here is retuned for the guitar you've set up, and the sheet shows exactly what changed.

      Spark 2 presets don't translate

      Spark 2, LIVE and GO presets assume different speakers, and some use gear the Spark 1 doesn't have. Only Spark 1 models are used here, including the 2021 additions (ODS 50, Blues Boy, Insane 6508, Clone Drive, Guitar EQ). Update your amp's firmware if you don't see them.
